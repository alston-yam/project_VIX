# Pitch claims log

_Generated by `python -m vrp.pipeline --stage stats` from `stats_tables.csv`. Do not edit by hand; change the code or config and re-run._

**Sign convention: implied − realised.** Positive = the VIX over-forecast and a variance seller made money. (Carr & Wu use the opposite sign.)

**Rule for a claim to be usable:** for every piece of evidence the 95% confidence interval excludes the null in the claimed direction, and the largest p-value behind the claim, after a Holm–Bonferroni correction across all 18 candidate claims, is below 0.05. Confidence intervals use Newey–West (6 lags) or a circular block bootstrap (6-month blocks, 10,000 resamples), so they allow for autocorrelation between months. p-values shown as '≤ 0.0001' are at the resampling floor.

Sample: 267 months, October 2003 to December 2025 (VIX at the close of the first trading day of each month vs S&P 500 realised variance over the next 21 trading days). The payoff is an idealised one-month variance swap, held to expiry, with no costs.

## Standing cautions for every slide

1. State the sign (implied − realised) and the unit (scaled = fraction of implied variance; raw = annualised variance; spread = vol points) on every number.
2. Sell-off results (Test 3) condition on what the S&P did **during** the window. They show *when* the seller loses; they are not a signal available at t.
3. The payoff is an idealised variance swap. The VIX itself is not tradable; VIX futures and ETPs earn a different premium. No transaction costs, margin or position-sizing are modelled.
4. VIX values before 22 Sept 2003 are back-calculated by Cboe. Quote the post-2003 result (Test 5) next to the full-sample one.
5. A non-significant result is not proof of no effect (e.g. 'slope not different from 1' or a raw-variance mean that is not significant post-2003).
6. Literature numbers (Carr & Wu, BTZ) come from other samples and other sign or unit conventions; see `docs/primer.md` before quoting them next to ours.
