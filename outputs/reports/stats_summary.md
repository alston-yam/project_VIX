# v2 statistical tests: summary

_Generated by `python -m vrp.pipeline --stage stats`. Do not edit by hand._

**Sign convention: implied − realised.** Positive = the VIX over-forecast and a variance seller made money. (Carr & Wu use the opposite sign.)

Units: `payoff_var` = annualised variance (decimal); `payoff_scaled` = fraction of implied variance (1 − RV/IV); `vol_spread_pts` = vol points (VIX − realised vol).

Sample: 432 months (1990-01 to 2025-12), VIX at the close of the first trading day of each month vs realised variance from returns t+1 … t+21. All p-values are two-sided; all CIs are 95%. Bootstrap: circular blocks of 6 months, 10,000 resamples, seed 20261122. p-values shown as '≤ 0.0001' are at the resampling floor.

**Pitch claims:** 17 of 23 candidate claims are supported after a Holm correction; see `pitch_claims.md`.

Skewness and excess kurtosis (bias-corrected sample moments) with bootstrap CIs; the D'Agostino and Anscombe–Glynn p-values assume independent months, so the bootstrap CI is the robustness check. Mean − median is a robust lopsidedness measure that does not depend on higher moments. The tail count compares months below mean − 3·sd with what a normal distribution would give.

## Test 4. Is the VIX an unbiased forecast? (Mincer–Zarnowitz)

Regress realised on implied: RV_{t→t+21} = a + b·IV_t + e. The only regressor is the VIX at the close of t (checked in code by `require_known_at_t`). An unbiased forecast has a = 0 and b = 1; we test that jointly with a Newey–West Wald test, in variance, in vol and in log variance (logs dampen the crash months).
